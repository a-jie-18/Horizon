### [China&\#x27;s Overlooked Game Consoles: A Hardware History](https://sspai.com/post/113842) ⭐️ 8.0/10

rss · 少数派 \(生活方式与效率\) · Sep 9, 07:03

**「Background」** Popular nostalgia writing about Chinese game consoles usually stops at Subor, and the 2000s console ban is often blamed for stalling the industry. The author argues both views are incomplete: Chinese-language console manufacturing began in the late 1970s in Hong Kong, Macau, and Taiwan, then spread to the mainland in the early 1980s, and continued through education and export markets even during the ban.

**「Solution」** Tracing six phases from the 1970s onward, the author shows a continuous but overlooked lineage. VTech, founded in Hong Kong in 1976, built Pong-like machines, the CreatiVision \(with BASIC and an early isometric 3D game\), and later the education-focused Socrates, whose Video Painter brought professional video-editing functions down to roughly $100. Taiwan&\#x27;s Pushze built 6502-based BIT-60/BIT-90 hybrids, then the Gamate handheld, which matched GameBoy&\#x27;s price and hardware but was limited by a nearly first-party-only library; its successor, UMC subsidiary Dunhuang&\#x27;s 16-bit Super A&\#x27;Can, used a Motorola 68000 with in-house graphics and sound chips but arrived at the end of the 16-bit era and was squeezed by cheap Famicom clones and next-gen 3D consoles. In the 2000s, design and manufacturing shifted to the mainland, where SunPlus chips powered Wii-like motion consoles and VTech&\#x27;s V.Smile, while V.R. Technology extended 6502/PPU architecture. Game MP4 players then became the cradle of open-source handhelds: the Dingoo A320 and Gemei X760+ shared a JZ4732-based platform, but Dingoo treated it as a software platform while ODM parent Huaxinfei treated it as fixed-function hardware, a conflict that ended their partnership. Android handhelds like the JXD S7100 \(around 599 yuan\) combined emulator and mobile-game ecosystems, and GPD Win&\#x27;s roughly $710,000 Indiegogo campaign created the Windows handheld category, contrasting with the failed Smash Z. Maker consoles such as GameShell, Meowbit, and GameGo followed, all dependent on Shenzhen supply chains.

**「Takeaway」** The author concludes that this history reveals persistent weaknesses—strong hardware but weak original software and IP, and path dependency on ODM branding—rooted in the divide between hardware-seller thinking and platform thinking. Yet the same dense supply chain gives Chinese developers an &quot;unfair advantage&quot; for novel interactive hardware, even as policy still favors online games over consumer electronics.

### [A Small-Handed User&\#x27;s Year With iPhone 17 Pro Max](https://sspai.com/post/114366) ⭐️ 6.0/10

rss · 少数派 \(生活方式与效率\) · Sep 9, 09:30

**「Background」** The author, a self-described small-handed user, spent four years on an iPhone 13 mini and treats 70 mm width as the threshold for comfortable one-handed use. With compact phones effectively gone, they deliberately bought an orange iPhone 17 Pro Max in October 2025 to test why so many people prefer large phones.

**「Solution」** The experiment produced a genuinely capable device that the author finds hard to write about because it is boring in the best sense: a 12GB RAM flagship that never stutters, never overheats, and never shows UI glitches, so its power becomes invisible. Battery life was the clearest win—daily use settled around 65%, and the power banks that once accompanied every outing stayed home, with battery health still at 100% after 321 days and 234 cycles. The camera also benefited from the larger body: the 1/2.55&quot; telephoto and &quot;optical-quality zoom&quot; produce usable photos up to roughly 200 mm equivalent, and the screen finally provided the mid-size display the author wanted, cutting iPad Pro use and making the Dynamic Island feel elegant. But the ergonomic costs are concrete: 233 g plus a case approaches half a jin, the 78 mm width exceeds 80 mm with a case, one-handed use is impossible, the left-side speaker is blocked in landscape, and the Camera Control button is awkward and easy to mis-touch. Accessories became abundant but were mostly bought to fix grip problems they could not solve, so the phone often stayed in a pocket.

**「Takeaway」** The author concludes that the iPhone 17 Pro Max is an excellent all-around tool that they enjoyed using but still do not like, because for a small-handed user the size and weight constantly assert themselves. Their ideal device would combine a genuinely large screen with a body narrow enough to hold, a combination the current market does not allow.
