### [Why Racing Safety Depends on a Four-Stage Relay](https://sspai.com/post/114836) ⭐️ 8.0/10

rss · 少数派 \(生活方式与效率\) · Oct 16, 06:24

**「Background」** Two recent Chinese circuit crashes—a GT3 fire at Shanghai in September and a driver breaking three ribs at Guangdong in October—raised the same question casual viewers always ask: how does a driver walk away from a car torn to pieces? The author argues the answer is not luck but a deliberate safety chain, one whose components were each rejected by the racing world before being proven.

**「Solution」** The author frames modern crash survival as a four-leg relay. First, the car sacrifices itself: outer shells, suspension and wheels crush and fly off while the survival cell stays rigid, stretching the deceleration time so the body absorbs less. Grosjean&\#x27;s 2020 Bahrain crash at 192 km/h and 67G is the showcase—his power unit broke away and the nose crushed progressively, keeping the impact survivable. Second, HANS, a carbon shoulder yoke with straps to the helmet, redirects the neck&\#x27;s load into the torso; developed by Jim Downing and Robert Hubbard after a 1981 fatality, it sold only 250 units in its first decade amid mockery, and became mandatory in F1 only in 2003, nine years after Ratzenberger and Senna died at Imola. Third, the Halo and roll hoop protect the head: the titanium Halo, derided as a &quot;flip-flop&quot; and opposed by nine of ten teams, deflected Alonso&\#x27;s car off Leclerc at Spa in 2018 \(peak load estimated near 58 kN\) and held off a barrier for Grosjean; at Silverstone 2022 Zhou Guanyu&\#x27;s roll hoop was sheared off, and the Halo alone preserved the gap between helmet and ground. Fourth, trackside rescue actually saves lives. The author stresses that hardware only buys seconds: at Shanghai, rival driver Loek Hartog pulled the unconscious Ollie Millroy from the burning car in 59 seconds, while the chief medical officer arrived at 4:08 and the first ambulance at 6:16—prompting teams to withdraw and drawing criticism from Wu Yanzu. The author notes FIA&\#x27;s 2026 rules add two-stage nose absorption, higher side-intrusion loads and flashing warning lights, and cites a 2026 Chinese commerce-ministry notice on raising event-safety standards, while cautioning that some regulatory claims rest on secondary reporting.

**「Takeaway」** The author&\#x27;s thesis is that equipment buys time but only a trained, well-drilled rescue system converts that time into survival—so a driver&\#x27;s escape is never a miracle, but the product of engineers who once faced ridicule, sacrificial hardware, and rescue crews racing the clock.
