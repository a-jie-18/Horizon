### [ArkFlow: Turning Bank Cards into a Collection Wall](https://sspai.com/post/112442) ⭐️ 6.0/10

rss · 少数派 \(生活方式与效率\) · Aug 25, 03:13

**「Background」** Managing over 50 financial accounts across banks, brokerages, and e-wallets, the author found that traditional bookkeeping apps and spreadsheets failed to address his core need: not tracking every expense, but knowing what accounts he has, where his money is, and what he must not forget. This frustration led him to build ArkFlow, a personal finance app.

**「Solution」** Initially, ArkFlow focused on a &\#x27;life freedom&\#x27; asset system with 60 growth stages, but after launch, users ignored it and instead loved the card collection feature. The author realized that users value tangible, playful interactions over abstract analytics. He redesigned the app to center on a card wallet, allowing users to upload real card faces, stack, slide, and arrange cards into a wall. He implemented three stacking styles, including an Apple Wallet-like interaction, and spent significant effort on animation details to make sliding feel satisfying. The app also includes practical features like recording balances, credit limits, and due dates, but deliberately avoids bank syncing to protect privacy, storing data locally with iCloud sync. The author used AI tools \(Codex and Claude Code\) for development, which sped up coding but required his judgment for product decisions. He also noted the costs: about $1050 in two months, with ongoing subscription fees exceeding current revenue.

**「Takeaway」** The author concludes that for a tool app, delight can be a feature, and that a narrow, well-crafted experience—like turning bank cards into a collection—can be more valuable than a comprehensive but generic solution. He now sees ArkFlow not just as asset management but as a way to preserve memories and digital objects.

### [Match Their Size, Then Add One](https://www.reddit.com/r/selfimprovement/comments/1vycrjb/are_you_a_zero_or_a_hundred/) ⭐️ 5.0/10

reddit · r/selfimprovement · /u/Possible\_Score6395 · Aug 25, 21:13

**「Background」** In a self-improvement post, the author observes that people often struggle with how much to share about themselves in conversation, falling into two extremes: &\#x27;zeros&\#x27; who share nothing and &\#x27;hundreds&\#x27; who overshare. Both approaches fail because they decide unilaterally how much to give, ignoring what the other person signals.

**「Solution」** The author proposes a simple rule: &\#x27;match their size, then add one.&\#x27; This means mirroring the other person&\#x27;s level of disclosure and adding a single detail, then stopping. For example, if they mention a rough week, you share a similar small struggle, not a dramatic story. This approach lets the other person decide whether to go deeper, as they will ask follow-up questions if interested. The author offers practical tips: keep it to one sentence, share small true things rather than impressive ones, and for zeros, share preferences; for hundreds, save big stories for the third question. The test is whether the other person asks a follow-up, indicating the size was right.

**「Takeaway」** The author&\#x27;s core thesis is that effective self-disclosure is about calibrating to the other person&\#x27;s cues, not about a fixed amount. By offering one step at a time and letting the other person choose to come closer, you build connection without overwhelming or withdrawing.
