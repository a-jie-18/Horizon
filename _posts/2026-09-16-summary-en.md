### [Student Finance Advice: Budgeting Before Investing](https://sspai.com/post/47126) ⭐️ 4.0/10

rss · 少数派 \(生活方式与效率\) · Sep 15, 03:10

**「Background」** Many students arrive at university with no stable income, no capital, and finances still controlled by their parents, so the usual goal of personal finance—growing capital—doesn&\#x27;t really apply yet. The author, writing from his own experience starting to manage money in university, argues that what students actually lack is a sense of planning around income and spending.

**「Solution」** His central insight is that students should treat budgeting, not earning, as the priority, built on three practices. First, adopt fully independent accounting: count tuition, housing, phones, computers, and all large purchases as your own expenses drawn from your allowance, rather than asking family for extra money whenever a big cost appears—a habit the author says later turns into credit-card installments and consumer loans once you&\#x27;re working. Second, have the allowance paid in a fixed amount on a fixed cycle, ideally yearly since tuition and housing run on annual cycles, and at minimum monthly; the amount should cover all academic and living costs, and the cycle should be as long as possible, though the author notes some students given a year&\#x27;s money at once spend it within a month, which he attributes to the abrupt shift from strict parental control rather than pure self-control. Third, keep accounts, because you cannot plan future spending without knowing past spending; under a year, a ledger mainly just tells you what you spent, but beyond a year it covers annual expenses like tuition, insurance, and memberships. He adds a checklist: estimate yearly total costs, choose yearly or monthly disbursement, get family support and stop requesting extra funds, set a monthly budget excluding tuition, optionally lock tuition in a principal-protected product, and track spending with software such as MoneyWiz. On timing, he argues starting in university is not early: reaching competence takes roughly 3–5 years or at least one market cycle, fund minimums are now often under 10 yuan, and the &quot;tuition&quot; paid for investing mistakes is proportional to capital, so losing small amounts early is cheaper than learning later with more money.

**「Takeaway」** The author&\#x27;s thesis is that students should start with planning and independent accounting rather than chasing returns, since once they have independent accounting and a fixed periodic allowance they are essentially no different from salaried workers. Starting early, he concludes, means earlier gains and cheaper mistakes.
