### [vivo X500 Pro Max: Making Video as Casual as Photos](https://sspai.com/post/115456) ⭐️ 4.0/10

rss · 少数派 \(生活方式与效率\) · Oct 9, 06:43

**「Background」** Smartphones now reliably handle communication, interaction, and performance, so the author&\#x27;s real question is whether a phone can become a dedicated tool for a specific scenario — for him, vivo&\#x27;s role as a pocket camera. He argues that since the X300 Ultra, vivo has moved past pure spec and algorithm competition toward serving aesthetic expression, encouraging users to get color right before shooting so images come out usable straight from the camera.

**「Solution」** The X500 Pro Max extends that &quot;straight-out&quot; philosophy from stills to video, aiming to fold complex cinematic grading into the moment you press record. On a trip to Kaili, the author abandoned his plan to shoot Log and exploit the new LOFIC sensor&\#x27;s claimed 17EV dynamic range, instead shooting entirely in the &quot;Classic Film&quot; Film Style. He credits that preset with restrained highlights and shadows, soft transitions on water and old walls, and colors that don&\#x27;t fight each other, giving ordinary scenes a quiet narrative feel. He treats stabilization and audio as the invisible skeleton: stabilization should absorb harsh micro-shakes while keeping natural breathing room, and the phone&\#x27;s clean, layered audio preserved foreground and background — notably in a dim, noisy bar where the image wasn&\#x27;t artificially brightened and noise reduction didn&\#x27;t flatten the vocalist against surrounding chatter. For stills, the &quot;Humanities&quot; mode offers 24mm/35mm/50mm focal scales and shutter-priority control, and the new &quot;Humanities Silver Salt&quot; black-and-white preset mimics darkroom paper with a faint warm tone, deep shadows, delicate midtone gradation, and fine grain. The new &quot;digital intermediate&quot; format is described as a still Log photo: in-camera noise reduction, distortion correction, and dynamic-range compositing are done first, while contrast curve and saturation are left open for later LUTs or grading — a middle ground the author says RAW struggles to occupy on phones. He concedes the workflow is still limited and &quot;toy-like.&quot;

**「Takeaway」** The author&\#x27;s thesis is that a phone camera&\#x27;s most important job is removing barriers — burying dynamic range, stabilization, audio, and color handling in the background so shooters can act with confidence and without a backlog of unfinished footage. Whether that claim holds beyond his anecdotal Kaili trip remains unverified by the piece.

### [Anyway.Fonts: A Chinese-Friendly Font Preview Tool](https://sspai.com/post/114869) ⭐️ 4.0/10

rss · 少数派 \(生活方式与效率\) · Oct 9, 02:08

**「Background」** Designers now have access to far more Chinese fonts than a decade ago, but these fonts vary widely in language version and character set, making management and comparison harder. Existing preview tools, the author notes, are often incomplete or pay too little attention to Chinese typography.

**「Solution」** The author built Anyway.Fonts, a browser-based font previewer aimed at Chinese users. Its standout feature filters fonts by whether they can fully render a given phrase—for example, a wedding invitation—so users can first eliminate fonts with missing glyphs, then compare style. The author admits this does not solve the underlying problem and says an AI glyph-completion Codex plugin was tried but abandoned because results were not good enough and copyright questions remained unresolved. The tool also offers vertical-layout previews, alongside list and grid modes, to show how text and punctuation behave in vertical web typography. Poster-style previews with over a dozen AI-generated templates—headlines, body text, vertical layouts, two-column and ticket formats—let users see fonts in design contexts; the author added personal touches and tuned details over time. Other features include side-by-side, line-by-line, or overlapping comparison of up to four fonts, style filtering, Chinese-friendly preset sample text, variable-font filtering, favorites, account sync, and Chinese names shown first. It is free, needs no install or login, and works in desktop Chrome or Edge; Safari is unsupported because it cannot read third-party fonts. Font files are parsed locally and not uploaded.

**「Takeaway」** Anyway.Fonts is a free, local-first previewer that prioritizes Chinese font coverage and vertical typography, though its AI glyph-completion idea remains an unresolved experiment. It mainly serves designers who want a more Chinese-aware way to compare fonts.
