### [F1 Safety Car: Why the Fastest Cars Follow a Slow Leader](https://sspai.com/post/113877) ⭐️ 7.0/10

rss · 少数派 \(生活方式与效率\) · Sep 7, 07:00

**「Background」** In Formula 1, when an accident or debris endangers drivers but the race isn&\#x27;t severe enough to stop, a safety car leads the pack at reduced speed. This seems paradoxical: the fastest cars in the world must queue behind a much slower vehicle, and its deployment can erase hard-earned leads or gift free pit stops. The author explains the rules, procedures, and strategic tradeoffs of safety cars, using historical races to illustrate their impact.

**「Solution」** The article breaks down the three ways F1 slows a race: the physical safety car \(SC\), the virtual safety car \(VSC\), and the red flag. The SC compresses the field into a single line, while VSC uses speed limits without a physical leader, and red flags pause the race entirely. The author details the full sequence of a safety car deployment: deployment, queuing \(with a 10-car gap, extendable to 20 in poor visibility\), pit stops \(allowed mainly for tire changes\), and unlapping of lapped cars. The central insight is that safety cars create a &\#x27;free pit stop&\#x27; window—pitting during SC/VSC costs about 12 seconds instead of 21, saving 9 seconds—but also compress gaps, negating leads. Teams face a narrow decision window: pit for fresh tires or stay out to preserve track position. The 2021 Abu Dhabi finale exemplifies this: Verstappen pitted for soft tires while Hamilton stayed out on old hards, leading to Verstappen&\#x27;s overtake on the last lap. Conversely, the 2026 British GP showed staying out can protect a lead if the race ends under SC. The author also notes safety cars are increasingly frequent: in 2026, every race so far has had at least one SC/VSC deployment. Historical examples include the 1973 Canadian GP&\#x27;s first safety car error, the 2008 Singapore &\#x27;crashgate&\#x27; scandal, and the 2023 Australian GP&\#x27;s three red flags.

**「Takeaway」** The author concludes that the safety car is not just a safety measure but a strategic wildcard that can decide championships, as seen in Abu Dhabi 2021. Understanding its rules and tradeoffs is essential for any F1 fan, as its frequency in modern racing means it often shapes race outcomes.

### [The Morning Routine That Backfired](https://www.reddit.com/r/selfimprovement/comments/1w9oznc/my_10step_morning_routine_was_actually_ruining_my/) ⭐️ 4.0/10

reddit · r/selfimprovement · /u/Spartan\_11V · Sep 7, 10:58

**「Background」** A Reddit user describes how a year of following online productivity trends—waking at 5:30 AM, drinking mineral water with salt, meditating, taking cold showers, journaling, and tracking habits—left him exhausted and anxious before his workday even began. The rigid routine turned waking up into an unpaid corporate onboarding process, where any deviation triggered guilt and made him feel like a failure.

**「Solution」** The author&\#x27;s central insight was that the routine itself was the problem, not his discipline. After abandoning the entire routine and switching to a simpler start—waking at 7:30 AM, wearing sweatpants, drinking tap water, and opening his laptop—he found that his work output did not drop at all, while his morning anxiety completely disappeared. He realized that what he had interpreted as a lack of discipline was actually exhaustion from trying to live like a lab experiment before breakfast. The key mechanism was removing the arbitrary checkboxes that had made his mood dependent on completing eight tasks, allowing him to start the day without the mental burden of guilt.

**「Takeaway」** The author&\#x27;s core thesis is that elaborate productivity routines can become counterproductive, causing more harm than good when they drain energy and create guilt. He questions why the internet convinced people that existing normally is a character flaw, suggesting that simplicity and self-compassion may be more sustainable than rigid optimization.
