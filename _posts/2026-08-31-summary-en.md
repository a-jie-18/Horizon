### [Counting the Uncounable: Spoon-billed Sandpiper Population Estimates](https://sspai.com/post/113827) ⭐️ 6.0/10

rss · 少数派 \(生活方式与效率\) · Aug 31, 02:53

**「Background」** The spoon-billed sandpiper is a critically endangered bird with only a few hundred individuals left, yet published population estimates vary widely—from 250 to 490. This discrepancy confuses conservationists and the public alike, prompting the author to explain why such variation exists and how scientists arrive at these numbers.

**「Solution」** The author uses a book market analogy to demystify population estimation methods. Direct counting, like scanning a market, yields a rough upper bound. Proportional estimation multiplies a sampled ratio by total market size, but is imprecise. Mark-recapture, the core method for this species, tags a sample and uses the recapture ratio to estimate total population—yielding about 480 in the analogy. Advanced models like Jolly-Seber and Pradel estimate survival and recruitment but require more data than available. Statistical modeling with bootstrap provides a confidence interval \(443, range 331–554\), while Leslie matrix and GLM account for age and site differences. AICc model selection reveals a 5% annual decline. The author contrasts this with the black-faced spoonbill, which is easier to count via global synchronized direct counts due to its larger size and visibility, emphasizing that method choice depends on species traits.

**「Takeaway」** The author concludes that while the exact number of spoon-billed sandpipers remains uncertain, the critical takeaway is the trend: a 5% annual decline, which could halve the population in about 13 years. Accurate counting is vital for conservation, but the real urgency lies in addressing habitat loss and other threats along their migratory route.
