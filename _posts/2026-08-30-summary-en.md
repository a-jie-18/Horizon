### [Tricking Your Brain into Confidence](https://www.reddit.com/r/selfimprovement/comments/1w2oeik/how_i_trick_my_brain_into_becoming_confident/) ⭐️ 4.0/10

reddit · r/selfimprovement · /u/ChucklesMuffin · Aug 30, 17:55

**「Background」** The author struggled with social anxiety in high-pressure situations, where the fear of appearing anxious only amplified the anxiety. This created a cycle that made normal interactions feel overwhelming.

**「Solution」** The author&\#x27;s trick is to mentally adopt a confident persona before entering a stressful interaction. For example, before meeting his boss, he imagined being his boss&\#x27;s boss, casually dropping by rather than being summoned. This shift in perspective instantly changed his demeanor—he felt calm, spoke clearly, and listened without overthinking. He later used variations, like imagining himself as a wealthy businessman who just closed a huge deal, to handle conversations with strangers. The key is not arrogance but temporarily stepping into a character that embodies confidence, which removes the pressure and panic. Over time, this role-play teaches the brain what confidence feels like, making it less of a pretense and more of a learned skill.

**「Takeaway」** The author concludes that confidence can be cultivated by consciously adopting a confident persona, which eventually becomes second nature. This personal anecdote suggests that mental reframing can break the cycle of anxiety and build genuine self-assurance.

### [Overcoming Social Discomfort One Small Step at a Time](https://www.reddit.com/r/selfimprovement/comments/1w2qwrs/if_talking_to_people_makes_you_uncomfortable_try/) ⭐️ 4.0/10

reddit · r/selfimprovement · /u/yaboythewiseman · Aug 30, 19:29

**「Background」** The author, who discovered they were autistic at age 22, struggled with eye contact and social interactions for years. Rather than attempting to master the entire social skill set at once, they sought a more manageable approach to gradually build comfort.

**「Solution」** The author&\#x27;s method is to focus on the single smallest obstacle holding them back on any given day. They started by practicing eye contact until it became natural, then moved on to giving genuine smiles, then to saying hello, and eventually to cultivating genuine curiosity. This incremental approach, likened to slowly entering a cold pool, allowed them to adapt to social situations without overwhelming themselves. The key is to identify the smallest step you can take today and practice it daily until it becomes comfortable, then move on to the next challenge.

**「Takeaway」** The author&\#x27;s core thesis is that social anxiety can be overcome by breaking down the daunting task of socializing into tiny, manageable steps, focusing on one at a time. This approach makes progress feel achievable and sustainable, leading to gradual improvement and eventual comfort in social settings.
