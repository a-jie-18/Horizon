### [Sony&\#x27;s Mini-Laptop Line: Picturebook to VAIO P](https://sspai.com/post/114821) ⭐️ 5.0/10

rss · 少数派 \(生活方式与效率\) · Oct 12, 03:20

**「Background」** After Toshiba&\#x27;s 1996 Libretto 20 launched the mini-laptop category, Japanese makers raced to shrink full computers, and Sony answered with the 1998 VAIO PCG-C1 Picturebook. The recurring constraint was that small size, cooling, battery life, and performance could not all be satisfied at once.

**「Solution」** The author traces four Picturebook generations: the 1998 PCG-C1 with a Pentium MMX 233MHz, an 8.9-inch 1024x480 ultrawide screen, a rotating camera, and about 1kg; the 1999 PCG-C1X moving to Pentium II or Celeron with Jog Dial and dual speakers; the 2000 third generation switching to Transmeta Crusoe; and the 2001 fourth generation with a 1280x600 screen and Crusoe TM5800 or TM5600. The author&\#x27;s own PCG-C1XA runs a Pentium II 400MHz, 64MB RAM, 12GB HDD, and Windows 98 SE, while a PCG-C1MR/BP uses the older TM5600 and Windows XP Home, which the author found noticeably laggy when opening documents or music. The author attributes this to Crusoe not natively supporting x86: its Code Morphing software layer dynamically translates x86 instructions into VLIW instructions at runtime, causing overhead and first-run loading delays, so real performance is typically only 60-80% of a same-clock Pentium III, with Transmeta claiming the 667MHz TM5600 roughly matched a Pentium III 500MHz. The author speculates, without an official Sony explanation, that Sony chose Crusoe because Pentium III-M ran too hot and power-hungry for a 1kg fanless body. The classic clamshell form then vanished for eight years, the author argues, because high-clock, multi-core PC processors and stagnant batteries made a pocketable full-keyboard laptop impractical until Intel&\#x27;s 2008 Atom cut power to two or three watts. The 2009 VAIO P revived the ultrawide screen and clamshell keyboard, but its Atom chip sparked debate over whether it was a laptop or a netbook; Sony marketed it as a Lifestyle PC with an 8-inch 1600x768 screen, colors, and pocketability. The author&\#x27;s VGN-P610 has an Atom Z520, 1GB RAM, 80GB HDD, and Windows XP, and its Linux-based Instant Mode boots in about 20-30 seconds to an XMB-like interface for photos, music, browsing, and chat with better battery life. The 2010 VPC-P118JC adds a tiny right-bezel touchpad, an ambient light sensor, and a 64GB SSD with an Atom Z540 and 2GB RAM, replacing Instant Mode with Quick Web Access; the author prefers the first generation&\#x27;s richer instant system and notes the pointing stick could drift over time.

**「Takeaway」** Across Picturebook and VAIO P, Sony repeatedly showed that making computers smaller meant sacrificing performance, and the author sees both lines as kindred answers to the same impossible tradeoff rather than a formal product succession.

### [派评 Roundup: Six Apps for macOS, Android, and iOS](https://sspai.com/post/115934) ⭐️ 4.0/10

rss · 少数派 \(生活方式与效率\) · Oct 12, 09:30

**「Background」** This installment of 派评 is a multi-app roundup covering six recent releases across macOS, Android, and iOS, spanning system monitoring, status-bar interaction, Apple hardware cataloging, desktop virtualization, PC gaming on Android, and concert photography. Each entry is a feature-and-pricing overview rather than a technical deep dive, so the piece is most useful to readers already curious about these specific apps.

**「Solution」** Vitals \(macOS, $15 one-time\) consolidates CPU, memory, disk, network, GPU, and battery metrics into a single window and menu bar, grouping processes by application rather than by process and offering an MCP integration that lets AI tools like Claude, Codex, and Cursor read system metrics via natural language. Tapbar \(Android, free and open source on F-Droid\) uses accessibility permissions to divide the status bar into up to four customizable tap regions with single, double, triple, and long-press actions, plus an app blacklist and landscape disable. Hoardware \(iOS/iPadOS 17+, macOS 14+, visionOS\) catalogs Apple hardware against the community AppleDB database, syncing via iCloud and treating firmware versions as a first-class field, with export to text, JSON, PDF, or image and read-only share links. VMPal \(macOS, currently in beta with full features free, personal license $34.50 per device\) is a buy-once virtualization tool that downloads OS images automatically, imports Parallels VMs, defaults to GPU acceleration, and exposes an AI agent option via MCP; the author notes it lacks Parallels&\#x27; coherence mode, positioning it for AI-driven rather than human app-level use. DroidDeck \(Android 9+, Adreno 730 or newer\) builds a Linux environment to run the Steam Linux client in Jupiter UI with Proton translation; the author measured roughly 12 GB of storage after installing a 1.5 GB game, and reports that lighter games run smoothly while 3A titles struggle. 饭拍相机 \(iOS, free with a 12 yuan monthly subscription\) offers four concert presets, limited manual controls, tap-to-lock subject tracking, three stabilization levels, and Live Photo support.

**「Takeaway」** The roundup&\#x27;s throughline is that small, focused utilities are carving out niches the platform defaults ignore—status bars that respond to touch, firmware as a collectible attribute, and virtualization aimed at AI agents rather than human desktop users. The author&\#x27;s evidence is largely hands-on impressions and store listings, so the claims remain useful starting points rather than measured benchmarks.

### [iOS 27 Parental Controls and Child Safety Features Overview](https://sspai.com/post/115242) ⭐️ 4.0/10

rss · 少数派 \(生活方式与效率\) · Oct 12, 07:00

**「Background」** Apple previewed iOS 27 and related systems at WWDC26 in June 2026, then shipped the new parental-control and child-safety features in September. Using them requires every device in a Family Sharing group to run the new OS versions and the organizer or parent to update the parental-control settings; otherwise families fall back to the old features or adjust settings directly on the child&\#x27;s device.

**「Solution」** The author describes the upgrade as centering on content restrictions, communication safety, browse requests, and time limits, with Screen Time redesigned and parental controls pushed into initial child-account setup. Child accounts are now created during first-time device setup: the parent scans an animation from their own device to join the child to Family Sharing, must verify adulthood \(in mainland China by adding a credit card to their Apple account payment method\), enters the child&\#x27;s birth date, agrees to privacy terms, and creates the account with an email plus a phone number. The author flags one practical warning: create the account with email first and add the phone number afterward, because doing it the other way can trigger a mysterious bug that disables some parental-control features. Content restrictions use an age filter from 4 and under to 21 that adjusts web, app, movie, TV, book, and Apple Media rules; under-18 accounts default to blocking adult websites, with age rules varying by region. Parents can choose which built-in apps a child may access during setup or later under Apps &amp; Websites, hide disallowed apps without deleting them, require approval for App Store downloads and in-app purchases, and block app deletion. The new Request to Browse feature makes Safari and other supported browsers ask a parent before a child opens a new site, similar to Ask to Buy and working across iPhone, iPad, and Mac. Communication Safety scans photos and videos in Messages, AirDrop, and FaceTime for nudity, gore, or violence, blurring them and offering help options; gore and violence detection does not apply to live FaceTime calls, only video messages, and the feature is on by default for under-18 users, with regional variation. Apple says the analysis runs on-device via machine learning, so it cannot see the results or access the media. Communication Limits let parents manage who a child can contact in Messages, FaceTime, and Phone, with separate rules for screen time and downtime, plus control over whether the child can edit contacts or must request approval. Screen Time now shows average usage and most-used apps at a glance and allows adjusting app and web limits directly; time allowances can be managed in batches by app category, and Apple offers expert-researched age-based duration suggestions. Downtime works like Focus, letting parents set different limits by scenario and time period, such as pausing games during school and allowing them on holidays.

**「Takeaway」** The author concludes that iOS 27 completes the parental-control and child-account system, shifting device management from restriction and monitoring toward system-level management and protection across time, apps, content, communication, and device use, giving parents more flexible rules by age and scenario.
