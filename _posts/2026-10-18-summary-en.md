### [Quantified Heartbeats: What HRV Can and Cannot Tell You](https://sspai.com/post/104231) ⭐️ 7.0/10

rss · 少数派 \(生活方式与效率\) · Oct 17, 06:58

**「Background」** After switching from an Apple Watch to an Oura Ring, the author—a medical student and gadget enthusiast—noticed both devices foreground heart rate variability \(HRV\) for stress, recovery, and sleep, and that a growing app ecosystem and even Garmin now build on it. That prompted a practical question: what HRV actually measures, how accurate consumer wearables are, and how the numbers should be interpreted.

**「Solution」** The author explains that HRV quantifies beat-to-beat variation in RR intervals, reflecting autonomic balance: sympathetic activation lowers HRV while parasympathetic \(vagal\) activity raises it. Consumer devices mostly use time-domain metrics because frequency and nonlinear analyses demand cleaner, longer signals than PPG can reliably provide. SDNN, the standard deviation of normal intervals, tracks overall variation and long-term cardiovascular risk but is distorted by heart-rate shifts; RMSSD, built from successive differences, is less affected by rate trends and captures fast vagal activity, making it better suited to recovery and sleep assessment. The author notes Apple Watch uses SDNN while Oura uses RMSSD, and that third-party apps can compute RMSSD from Apple’s raw data. On value, HRV is described as sensitive but nonspecific: a 2022 meta-analysis linked the lowest quartile to 1.56 times all-cause mortality risk, and lower HRV has been associated with 32–45% higher first cardiovascular event risk, but a low reading cannot identify the cause. On accuracy, PPG measures pulse-wave variation rather than true HRV, yet during nighttime rest agreement with ECG is high \(correlations 0.91–0.999\); a 2025 study ranked Oura Gen 4 above Oura Gen 3, WHOOP 4.0, Garmin Fenix 6, and Polar Grit X Pro, with rings outperforming wrist devices. Accuracy falls with exercise intensity and varies with skin tone, and because algorithms and sampling differ, values from different brands can differ by 200–300%, so only same-device trends are meaningful. The author recommends a personal baseline, consistent measurement conditions, and caution against reading high as good and low as bad.

**「Takeaway」** HRV is a useful longitudinal signal for spotting departures from your own baseline, not a diagnostic score or a cross-person comparison. The author’s larger point is that constant score-watching can turn a monitoring tool into a source of anxiety, and that data should support, not replace, direct bodily awareness.
