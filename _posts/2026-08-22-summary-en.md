### [Training Social Confidence: A Request for Real Progression Stories](https://www.reddit.com/r/selfimprovement/comments/1vvp11k/for_people_who_actually_trained_their_social/) ⭐️ 5.0/10

reddit · r/selfimprovement · /u/Past\_Significance751 · Aug 22, 21:51

**「Background」** The author notes that common advice on social confidence—&\#x27;just be yourself&\#x27; or &\#x27;talk to more people&\#x27;—is vague and unhelpful. They seek concrete, step-by-step accounts from people who were genuinely shy or awkward and improved through deliberate practice.

**「Solution」** The author asks for detailed progression narratives: starting points at their worst, such as eye contact, thanking a cashier, or initiating greetings, and how they knew when to escalate difficulty. They want to know if practitioners moved on to longer conversations, group settings, meeting new people, or dating. Crucially, they ask whether improvements stuck after periods of reduced practice, or if skills rusted. The post is a question, so it offers no answers or evidence itself, but it outlines a framework for tracking social skill development.

**「Takeaway」** The author&\#x27;s core point is that social confidence is built through a gradual, observable progression of challenges, and they want to understand the long-term durability of such training. This highlights the need for practical, experience-based guidance rather than generic platitudes.

### [Making Adult Friends: A Personal Strategy](https://www.reddit.com/r/selfimprovement/comments/1vvh59j/making_friends_as_an_adult_feels_impossible_heres/) ⭐️ 4.0/10

reddit · r/selfimprovement · /u/yaboythewiseman · Aug 22, 16:39

**「Background」** The author observes that making friends as an adult feels impossible, but argues that the same approach used at any age can work: consistent exposure through shared activities. The key challenge is moving from stranger to trusted friend, which the author claims typically takes three to six months of regular interaction.

**「Solution」** The author&\#x27;s strategy is to pick a recurring activity—such as a hobby, religion, or event—and engage with people there. He suggests starting with small talk, gradually sharing personal values to gauge alignment, and then proposing regular hangouts like hikes or go-karting. He emphasizes that consistency accelerates trust-building. If no existing group fits, he recommends starting your own tribe by inviting like-minded people, framing it as a public service since others are equally lonely. He shares his personal experience of being adopted by tribe leaders in college and now leading his own group in a metropolitan area.

**「Takeaway」** The author&\#x27;s core thesis is that adult friendship is achievable through deliberate, consistent exposure and proactive initiation, and that taking the lead in forming a community can benefit both oneself and others.
