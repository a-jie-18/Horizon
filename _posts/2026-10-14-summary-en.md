### [Apple Watch Edition: A Materials Lab for Apple](https://sspai.com/post/114561) ⭐️ 6.0/10

rss · 少数派 \(生活方式与效率\) · Oct 13, 03:09

**「Background」** Apple Watch Edition never played the volume role of the mainline Series or the ongoing partnership role of Hermès; instead, the author frames it as a multi-year experiment in what a &quot;high-end smartwatch&quot; could mean. The core tension: a device that ages like a phone struggles to carry the permanence and value of a mechanical watch, so Apple kept swapping materials to answer the same question.

**「Solution」** The author traces three answers. The 2015 first-generation Edition used 18K gold \(from ¥74,800, up to ¥126,800\), with a custom alloy claimed at twice standard gold&\#x27;s hardness, sold through department stores and fashion boutiques; it lasted one generation, and the author reads a 2018 Isetan clearance as a sign of weak demand. Series 2 \(2016\) shifted to zirconia-based precision ceramic at ¥9,588, moving the definition of &quot;premium&quot; from material price to manufacturing difficulty: powder pressing, roughly 35 hours of sintering near 1500°C, about 20% shrinkage that engineers had to pre-compensate for, then CNC with 70-plus diamond tools \(up to 6 hours\) and about 2 hours of polishing. Series 3 added gray ceramic, but Series 4 \(2018\) dropped Edition entirely while keeping ceramic only as a functional back for cellular performance. Edition returned in Series 5 \(2019\) with titanium alongside white ceramic, then Series 6 \(2020\) went titanium-only, and Series 7 \(2021\) was the last Edition. The author argues the logic migrated: titanium moved to Apple Watch Ultra \(2022\), then replaced stainless steel as the polished Grade 5 titanium option on Series 10 \(2024, nearly 20% lighter than Series 9 stainless\), and Series 11 \(2025\) moved titanium cases to 3D-printed mass production using 100% aerospace-grade recycled titanium powder, reportedly halving raw material per case. In 2026, Series 12 brings ceramic back as pearl white and night blue—no longer an Edition, just a case option—with ceramic snaps on matching sport bands echoing Edition&\#x27;s exclusive hardware.

**「Takeaway」** The author&\#x27;s thesis is that Edition failed as a luxury product line but succeeded as a materials and process testbed: gold, ceramic, and titanium each taught Apple something, and those lessons—especially titanium and additive manufacturing—now serve Ultra and the mainline Series. What Edition left behind, in this reading, is not a material but a way of developing materials.

### [Pikko: An Energy-Based Task App for &\#x27;P&\#x27; and ADHD Users](https://sspai.com/post/115400) ⭐️ 4.0/10

rss · 少数派 \(生活方式与效率\) · Oct 13, 07:00

**「Background」** The author, a longtime task-app tinkerer, argues that most task managers are built for MBTI Judging types who like planning, leaving Perceiving types and ADHD users stuck in a &quot;record but don&\#x27;t do&quot; loop: tasks pile up, and opening the list only produces guilt and paralysis. Yet the author still needed something to clarify what to do, so they built Pikko over about a month.

**「Solution」** Pikko replaces list-style management with energy-based matching. Instead of projects, times, and tags, each task is described by four attributes — energy cost, device, scene, and mood — which Pikko auto-assigns and the user can correct by tapping a card. A self-developed energy algorithm combines the user&\#x27;s sleep schedule, heart rate, and activity data into a Current Energy Level \(CEL\) shown on the home screen, with background colors shifting by state; with an Apple Watch it also uses sleep and heart-rate records to estimate focus windows. The list reorders in real time to surface tasks fitting the current state, showing at most three tasks until the user scrolls. Two completion gestures are offered: swipe right to finish, left to delete, or expand a card to start a focus timer. A receipt-printer view, reached by pulling down, prints a recommended set of main and side tasks; tearing it off starts focus timing. A geofenced &quot;focus fence&quot; prioritizes or de-emphasizes scenes based on location, so office hours don&\#x27;t surface home tasks. Time reminders are deliberately weakened — they can&\#x27;t be set manually and appear only when a task contains explicit time information. The author says the algorithm evolves locally with use and that CEL felt quite accurate in their own use, though no measured accuracy or failure data is given. Everything runs on-device with no data collection, and basic features are free, with receipt printer, geofencing, and stamp workshop behind a one-time Pikko Pro purchase; the best experience is currently iPhone-only, with iPadOS and macOS sync mentioned as possible future work.

**「Takeaway」** The author&\#x27;s thesis is that for P and ADHD users, the best task management may be no management at all: let an energy-aware, locally evolving system decide what fits right now rather than asking users to plan and prioritize. The piece is best read as a design sketch of that idea, since its central algorithm and accuracy claims rest on the author&\#x27;s own impressions rather than measured evidence.
