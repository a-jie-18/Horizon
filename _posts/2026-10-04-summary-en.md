### [CMF Clip Pro Open-Ear Clip Earbuds Review](https://sspai.com/post/114922) ⭐️ 6.0/10

rss · 少数派 \(生活方式与效率\) · Oct 3, 07:00

**「Background」** CMF, Nothing&\#x27;s sub-brand, released Clip Pro as its first clip-style open-ear \(OWS\) earbuds, positioned differently from the main brand&\#x27;s Nothing Ear \(Open\). In this hands-on review, TDS Studio evaluates it as a mid-range clip earbud, focusing on whether its design, battery, and sound justify the price.

**「Solution」** The author found the coral-orange design well-built, with a comfortable, easy-to-seat clip structure, IP54 earbuds, and a compact case whose signature dial is disabled by default and feels wobbly when spun. Controls rely on physical buttons with customizable single/double/triple/long presses, though prompts lag about a second and there is no wear detection. Connectivity is a mixed story: LDAC works but is unstable in signal-dense areas and only holds roughly 2m through a wall or 3m open in quality-priority mode, while AAC stays stable to about 10m; Google Fast Pair, Swift Pair, and dual-device connection coexist with LDAC. Measured battery with LDAC at 50% volume was 6h46m versus 10h37m on AAC, roughly matching or slightly exceeding the claimed 10h/32.5h. Sound is clean and balanced with moderate bass, slightly forward vocals, and a modest, well-behaved soundstage, but the author notes insufficient loudness at 50% on iPhone, a gimmicky spatial mode, and a leak-suppression mode that dulls highs.

**「Takeaway」** The author concludes the Clip Pro is a reasonably complete mid-range clip earbud with no glaring weaknesses, but its limited loudness and LDAC stability issues keep it from a top recommendation, especially against stronger-sounding rivals like the Xingge Yuanqi and Dunu LUNA. It earns a IV \(Recommend\) rating partly because CMF&\#x27;s pricing is restrained compared with phone-brand competitors.
