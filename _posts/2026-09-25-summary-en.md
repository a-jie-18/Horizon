### [Ten Years of the Antique Computer Room](https://sspai.com/post/114395) ⭐️ 6.0/10

rss · 少数派 \(生活方式与效率\) · Sep 24, 04:15

**「Background」** In 2016, author Peng An launched the &quot;Antique Computer Room&quot; column as a lean-startup MVP, testing whether the retrocomputing subculture thriving in the West could become a viable content business in China. The project grew out of an earlier PDA photo archive and a domestic collector culture that treated hardware as a scarcity contest judged by model, rarity, and condition.

**「Solution」** Over a decade, the author&\#x27;s stance shifted from hardware collector to media-archaeology practitioner. Two influences proved decisive: Seattle&\#x27;s Living Computers Museum, where vintage machines stay powered on and remotely accessible, showing that a computer without running software is little more than silicon and plastic; and the academic framework of Platform Studies \(Nick Montfort, Ian Bogost\), which treats a platform&\#x27;s hardware and software as shaping digital culture and art rather than as fetish objects. The author also drew lessons from contrasting publications: the humanities-heavy Offline magazine lacked hands-on technical grounding, while Shenzhen&\#x27;s Openbook skewed toward practice without cultural context, suggesting a successful project needs both. Finnish magazine Skrolli and the Digital Archaeology exhibition reinforced a content-first, chronological approach that foregrounds artworks while keeping hardware as background. Practically, this meant abandoning physical accumulation in favor of emulators, FPGA recreations, floppy emulators, and 3D-printed parts, culminating in the 2018 launch of the standalone &quot;Computer Museum in the Web&quot; site, inspired partly by the BBC Computer Literacy Project Archive and the popularity of online DOS games. The author summarizes retrocomputing as valuing experience over objects, content over specifications, and creativity over nostalgia.

**「Takeaway」** The author argues that retrocomputing&\#x27;s value lies not in owning and rating old hardware but in experiencing, studying, and re-creating the computing cultures those machines enabled, a shift that turned a personal hobby into serious media-archaeological practice.

### [Home Specialty Coffee and Drink Recipes](https://sspai.com/prime/story/home-made-beverages-5) ⭐️ 4.0/10

rss · 少数派 \(生活方式与效率\) · Sep 24, 08:20

**「Background」** This installment of a home-beverage series collects recipes for specialty coffee drinks and other cold beverages, aimed at readers who already make espresso at home. The author assumes a fixed espresso base and standard equipment, so the value lies in the ratios and assembly steps rather than in technique or explanation.

**「Solution」** The author recommends a dark-roast blend with nut, caramel, or cocoa notes so the espresso is not masked by mixers, and extracts a single shot from 18g of coffee in 25–27 seconds to yield 28–32g, testing each bean first. Moka pots, semi-automatic, and automatic machines are preferred; capsule machines are not recommended. Recipes are scaled for cups of at least 400ml, and jasmine tea is brewed in bulk at 50g tea to 1300g water at about 80°C for 7 minutes, stirred once, then filtered and combined with 600g ice. The drinks follow a common pattern: shake the liquid ingredients with a full cup of ice about ten times without over-diluting, pour over seven 20g ice cubes, and finish with cream or milk where specified. Examples include coconut evaporated-milk coffee, osmanthus coconut coffee, grape americano, lime iced americano, and a green-grape americano smoothie blended with fruit pulp jam. The author suggests buying fruit juices and pulp jams rather than juicing fresh fruit, and notes that the lime americano steps mistakenly repeat the grape ingredients.

**「Takeaway」** The author&\#x27;s core point is that consistent espresso extraction and a few fixed ratios are enough to reproduce café-style specialty drinks at home, provided you choose a robust dark-roast blend and avoid over-shaking the ice. The collection is practical but formulaic, and the copy-paste error in one recipe means readers should verify steps before relying on them.

### [Microsoft and DreamWorks&\#x27; Game Studio and Its Xbox Legacy](https://sspai.com/post/113823) ⭐️ 4.0/10

rss · 少数派 \(生活方式与效率\) · Sep 24, 07:06

**「Background」** By the mid-1990s Microsoft had spent heavily on multimedia and consumer software but still had few hit games beyond the aging Microsoft Flight Simulator, while Steven Spielberg wanted to push DreamWorks into interactive entertainment but needed technical and publishing muscle. In March 1995 the two announced DreamWorks Interactive, a 50/50 joint venture with $30 million in startup funding, splitting duties so DreamWorks supplied creative talent and Microsoft supplied technology and distribution.

**「Solution」** The author traces how the studio&\#x27;s uneven output nonetheless seeded later successes. Gates predicted hundreds of millions in sales within two to three years and more than twenty titles a year, but by 1997 only seven products had shipped, just four developed in-house, and the best seller, Goosebumps: Escape from Horrorland, reached about 130,000 US copies by August 1997 without turning a profit. The 1998 Jurassic Park action game Trespasser, led by Seamus Blackley and Austin Grossman, was ambitious—a physics engine for object and character interaction, one of the earliest wide-open 3D first-person shooters, AI-controlled dinosaurs—but rushed development, hardware limits, and disastrous physics produced mostly negative reviews and roughly 50,000 sales, with GameSpot calling it the worst game of 1998. Its clumsy controls later inspired comedy games such as Surgeon Simulator and Octodad. The 1999 Medal of Honor, born from Spielberg&\#x27;s World War II interest and his son&\#x27;s play of GoldenEye 007, became the studio&\#x27;s real success and a pioneer of WWII shooters. In February 2000 Electronic Arts bought the Los Angeles headquarters and took over Medal of Honor, while the Redmond portion returned to Microsoft. Blackley went on to push the Xbox project, crediting Trespasser&\#x27;s painful lesson as a major contributor to its creation; Alan Hartman moved through Digital Anvil, Turn 10, and eventually became head of Xbox Game Studios in 2023. EA Los Angeles continued Medal of Honor, and the 2015 studio that split from EA became Infinity Ward, whose Call of Duty the author and VG247 trace back through Medal of Honor: Allied Assault. Later, Medal of Honor: Above and Beyond returned to WWII in 2020 under Respawn Entertainment, led by Vince Zampella, while Call of Duty and Activision were acquired by Microsoft in 2023.

**「Takeaway」** The author argues that DreamWorks Interactive was a commercial failure by almost any direct measure—short-lived, few games, fewer hits—yet its indirect influence was deep: its titles helped define WWII shooters and physics-driven first-person games, and its alumni carried lessons into Xbox, Forza, Medal of Honor, Call of Duty, and Battlefield. In that sense, the studio&\#x27;s real success began after it ended, fulfilling Spielberg&\#x27;s stated ambition of making groundbreaking games without the DreamWorks Interactive logo attached.
