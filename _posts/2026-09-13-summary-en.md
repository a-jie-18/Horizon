### [A Job Seeker&\#x27;s Field Notes on AI-Era Hiring](https://sspai.com/post/114461) ⭐️ 4.0/10

rss · 少数派 \(生活方式与效率\) · Sep 12, 07:00

**「Background」** After his company cut over 40% of his team in a single four-hour Monday, the author spent a summer job hunting and still had not landed a role he wanted. He frames his account not as a success story but as a candid, anecdotal look at how AI has reshaped both job applications and the hiring process itself.

**「Solution」** The author&\#x27;s practical method centers on using AI as a writing and preparation partner rather than an autonomous agent. For resumes, he dictates every project detail to an AI, has it distill the material into a concise narrative, then feeds it several target job descriptions so the final version is tailored to matching roles; he insists on reviewing each analytical step to prevent the AI from over-interpreting his raw input. For interviews, he has the AI compare his resume against a JD to surface matches and gaps, generate likely questions around both, and only then fill in unfamiliar industry background. He also catalogs the hiring landscape he encountered: recruiters who only dial for quotas, condescending ones, and a rare efficient type; platform algorithms that auto-submit resumes at low priority and even place robotic cold calls; and roles that demand AI experience without defining it. He warns of opaque insurance &quot;reserve cadre&quot; posts and overseas jobs that may mask gray-market work, and describes interviews that were padded for headcount, unequal, or designed to extract his AI project details. One final-round rejection came after the company had initially said vertical experience was not required.

**「Takeaway」** The author concludes that today&\#x27;s hiring market is a seller&\#x27;s market where AI has made requirements more demanding and less transparent, so candidates should prepare deliberately with AI, push back on unreasonable demands, and refuse to waste time on bad-faith processes. His broader point is that no single job should define a life, and treating the search as one anchor among many makes starting over bearable.
